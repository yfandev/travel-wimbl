## Reading the results

- **The mechanics work.** A lesson written on trip A was stored, served to held-out trips within the token budget, and cited by ID (3 of 4 graded memory-on runs in the earlier run; 0 of 5 in the rerun).
- **No quality gain is shown.** Pooled deltas were +0.000 and -0.080 on small samples. Memory-on outputs also cost about 2.5x the output tokens and 2-3x the latency.
- **Likely cause: lesson quality, not plumbing.** Trip A itself scored 3/5 and missed the two checks the lessons should have taught (official resale channel, kickoff risk), so its self-written lessons are generic. Unvalidated candidate lessons are served to every trip immediately, so a weak lesson costs tokens without helping.
- **Rate limits.** Crusoe returned 429 `max_requests_limit` for Lightning too under 4 concurrent calls; failed calls are excluded from the scores above.

Small sample, one task type. Treat this as evidence the loop works end to end, not as a general quality claim.
